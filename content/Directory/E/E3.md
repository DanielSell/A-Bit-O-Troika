## Tallow Street: Vaulted Animarium (1)  
Julius Coldbridge (5/10/2/0)  
Grow  
Hamm Coldbridge (3/20/1/0)  
Track  
  
Spell: Befuddle  
  
Small shop full of small animals. Some move freely. The animals are so loud as to make it impossible to be heard from anywhere other than mouth to ear contact. Julius runs the shop with his adopted son Hamm, a shambling mound of a man who rarely tries to talk and is mostly deaf. Hamm has two rabbits living in his pockets. Ruby Lorgnettes in a drawer by the counter. Cabinet full of luminous larvae and singing beetles. Choir rats, various common city-owls, a nesting pig, 17 ogre fleas, a pair of sympathy serpents (not on speaking terms) are among the selection.  
  
  
  
## Ironspike Lane: Audience of Galaxis (2)  
Great Man Oannes Omophagle (5/12/2/0)  
Spells: Find, Web  
  
Outreach centre for a provincial cult. A small white-stone building with high metal fences preventing accidental contamination from the outside. Inside the exclusively male congregation preach all day through talking tubes into the street and inhaling spasmodic fumes all night. Their highly technical credo takes years to enter the mysteries of, but to the layman seems to centre on a rejection of mortal women as unclean and the requirement of writhing nightly in the erotic dreams of Galaxis.  
  
## Tallow Street: The Repose of Chocolate  
Chocolate Way    Ward (1)    4/9/2/1    E3 (3)    painter  
  
This used to be the home of the warlord-poet Grak but is now the home and studio of Chocolate Way, master landscape artist and immigrant from the Palace of Tigers & tiger. The spikes and chains have been removed, much to the local historical society's outcries, and replaced with wood paneling and the thickest carpeting this side of the river. Chocolate can often be seen scaling steeples and chimneys with his watercolours held in his mighty jaws, ready to paint his next masterpiece.   
  
## Behind Ironspike Lane: The New You  
Dollie Wolfgang    Fly    8/14/4/0    E3 (4)    Leatherseller  
  
A nondescript door below the shop sign gives very little away. This is out of respect for the possibl relatives of the deceased who may wander by. No one wants to see their aunt on a manakin in a window. Inside is full the skins of people who have sold their eventual remains to Dollie, the leatherworker. She turns the dead into fine suits, masks and costumes. They range from the full fantasy body suits with hidden zips and strategic elastication to reduce sagging, all the way down to novelty gloves and shoes. Everything is without exception expensive, being 50 to 100 times the price of a normal item of its sort.
